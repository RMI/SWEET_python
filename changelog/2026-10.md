# SWEET_python Changelog — October 2026

**Highlights:** Indonesia sizes its waste from its own row of the IPCC table
SWEET copies its per-capita waste rates from, instead of the South-Eastern Asia
default, which was 2.4 times higher (model-output change).

A site whose type nobody recorded is now guessed by Climate TRACE's rule
(income, region and named countries) in every SWEET path, instead of by one of
two region lists. 55 countries' unknown sites move from open dump to sanitary
landfill in WasteMAP's site tool (model-output change).

`load_csv_new` normalizes a city's composition, as the other
loaders do. WasteMAP's city DST loads a city from its published cities table
this way, and Brazil's precomputed rows there do not add up to 100% (São Paulo
99%, Brasília 87%). Every city DST scenario for those two cities was rejected as
"Invalid waste fractions" (model-output change).

A Custom Location city modelled no landfill methane at all in 31
countries, among them Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Indonesia, the Philippines,
Vietnam, South Africa, Canada and Germany. Its disposal split came out
(0, 0, 0), so its landfills took none of its waste. It now gets the split the
cities table gives a city with no landfill data (model-output change).

The City DST's "move waste to existing gas capture" lever now
changes a city's emissions. For a city on the generic landfill split, it worked
out a new split and never gave it to the landfills, so every setting returned the
no-change scenario. Moved to 90% gas capture, Lahore now emits 45% less in 2040
(model-output change).

The City DST's "add gas capture" lever now applies the oxidation
it sets, not just the gas capture. Its results fall another 10–13% after the
implementation year: a city that landfills everything ends up 65.3% below the
no-change scenario in 2040 instead of 60% (model-output change).

A Custom Location in Kosovo builds. `City.dst_baseline_blank`
raised "Country 'XKX' not found." for Kosovo's code, so WasteMAP's city tool
answered 500, and the site tool modelled a point in Kosovo as Serbia. SWEET now
looks a country up in its own table before asking pycountry, which has no Kosovo
(model-output change for a country given by name).

A blank or junk country is not found rather than modelled as the UK or Italy,
and the site tool's prefill finds the country for a point in Turkey, both Congos
and eight more places it used to fail on.

A city with no waste figure of its own now generates waste at
its country's per-capita default. `load_andre_params` used to move that default
from 2019 to the city's population year, although it already described the
population year, so the 39 cities that take this path came out up to 35% below
or 9% above the default (model-output change).

Waste sent to incineration stops producing methane. SWEET used to landfill 10%
of the burnable waste an incinerator received, unburned, as food, paper and so
on, and that 10% was most of the modelled methane of a city that incinerates
heavily: Yokohama's city DST methane falls 74% without it (model-output change).

## Added

- **A place's population by year can be worked out once and handed back.**
  `city_population_trajectory` gives a city's population in every model year, in
  people, and what its shape comes from: a UN-listed city's own two rates ("UN city
  rates") or its country's UN population ("country curve"), the choice
  `_city_growth` makes. `country_population_trajectory` gives a country's UN
  population, or for the 12 places with none the constant rate a Custom Location
  there grows by ("country fallback rates"). `load_csv_new` takes a stored
  trajectory as `population_series` and grows the city along it. WasteMAP's map
  build stores one per city and country, and its city DST hands a city's back, so
  the city DST cannot grow a city differently from the build behind it when
  WasteMAP's SWEET pin moves. Handed back, a trajectory grows a city as SWEET would
  have, to within about one part in 10^15: SWEET divides each year's population by
  the population-year figure, which can land a unit in the last place away from the
  factor it used to compute. An optional `city_points` argument takes the UN's own
  points for a city instead (constant growth between them); nothing uses it yet.
  No other number moves. ([#91](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/91),
  [RMI/WasteMAP#888](https://github.com/RMI/WasteMAP/pull/888))

- **A Custom Location can grow along a stored country trajectory.**
  `City.dst_baseline_blank` takes `population_series`, as `load_csv_new` does: the
  country's population by year from `country_population_trajectory`, stored with
  WasteMAP's map build. The Custom Location's waste, and its scenario's, grow along
  it instead of along SWEET's own table; the city's tonnage still comes from the
  population it is given, and the two growth rates a person reads are still the
  country's. Without it nothing changes. Handed the country's own trajectory, a
  Custom Location grows as before, to within 5.9e-16 in every country, and the 12
  with fallback rates exactly. ([#94](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/94),
  [RMI/WasteMAP#890](https://github.com/RMI/WasteMAP/pull/890))

- **Seven more Climate TRACE modelling decisions are marked where SWEET applies them**:
  waste before 1970 counted as zero, a site's last intake in the year before it closes,
  intake following population, the decay rate from climate, each waste type's methane
  potential, the monthly decay model and the flaring particulate factor. Comments only;
  no number moves.

- **The places SWEET applies a Climate TRACE modelling decision carry a comment
  naming it**, such as `# decision: mcf-by-type-and-depth`. The list of
  decisions is `docs/MODEL_DECISIONS.md` in RMI_Climate_TRACE_Waste_Methane, and
  its test checks these comments once a SWEET release with them is pinned there.
  Seven decisions are tagged: MCF, site type, waste composition, gas capture,
  oxidation, flaring and population growth. Comments only; no number moves.
  The README's "Decision comments" section explains them. A new pull-request
  template asks whether a change touches a tagged line.

## Changed

- **Nothing sent to an incinerator is landfilled unburned**
  (model-output change). `City.combustion_reject_rate` is 0, down from 0.1. The
  0.1 came from SWEET's Excel, whose "Reject Rate for Combustibles" is sourced as
  best professional judgement: 10% of the food, green waste, wood, paper,
  textiles and plastic sent to incineration went to landfill as that material and
  decayed. What an incinerator leaves is ash with almost no organic carbon. The
  EU's Industrial Emissions Directive (Art. 50) holds it under 3%, EPA's WARM
  counts no methane from it, IPCC's landfill guidance has no category for it, and
  Japan landfills under 1% of the waste it processes unburned.
  - Every path reads the rate: the city model behind WasteMAP's cities table and
    city DST, Custom Locations, Climate TRACE's site estimates, and the city-level
    advanced DST, where a `combusts` facility now deposits nothing and the
    combustion pathway diverts all it takes.
  - In the city DST, on the June 2026 cities table, the 32 of 5,962 cities that
    report incineration emit 8.5% less methane in 2025 between them. Oslo falls
    82%, Kobe 75%, Yokohama 74%, Budapest 66%, Stockholm 62%, London 22%, Delhi
    8% and Beijing 1%. No other city moves. A Custom Location moves where its
    country's default incinerates.
  - Open burning is a separate input and is untouched: `waste_burning` in
    `sdst_v1_5` and `implement_dst_changes_advanced` still takes its share out of
    the landfill and adds 3.7 g of CH4 per kg burned, the methane of the fire
    itself. No caller sets it today: WasteMAP's site tool always sends 0, and
    nothing in WasteMAP or Climate TRACE calls the second.
  - The rate is still a setting: a caller can set it on a `City`, and a
    `combusts` facility then deposits that share of its intake, as before.
  - Climate TRACE pins `sweet/2026.10.1`, so its numbers move only when it
    adopts a release with this. The new `# decision: combustion-reject` marks the
    setting for its `docs/MODEL_DECISIONS.md`.

  ([#100](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/100))

- **The composting and digestion methane factors say what units they are in**
  (docs only, no value changed). `_mef_compost`'s docstring said it was a methane
  factor "scaled to CO2e" by its ×1.1023×0.7, which made the later ÷28 look like
  a 28× undercount. It is CO2e at source: EPA WARM's fugitive composting CH4 in
  MTCO2E per short ton, via the SWEET Excel. ×1.1023 converts short tons to
  tonnes, and ×0.7 is the Excel's 30% evaporation loss. `mef_anaerobic` is
  methane, and the Excel notes it leaves out AD plant leakage.
- **A site with no recorded type is guessed the same way everywhere**
  (model-output change). The new
  `site_type_defaults.default_site_type(iso3, income_id=None)` holds the rule
  Climate TRACE's pipeline already used, moved unchanged from its
  `landfill_table_ops.fill_landfill_types`. A site is a sanitary landfill where
  its country is high income, or its region is in `landfill_default_regions`,
  Western Asia or Central Asia, or it is China, Russia, Mexico, Brazil or Costa
  Rica. Otherwise it is an open dump, and Mongolia and Yemen always are.
  - Income comes from the new `country_income.INCOME_ID_BY_ISO3`: the World Bank
    table Climate TRACE reads (What a Waste 2.0's country file, 217 countries),
    not the current World Bank list.
  - Before, SWEET guessed from the region alone, using one of two lists. One had
    Eastern Europe; the copy in `site_only_estimate` had Australia and New
    Zealand instead.
  - The site tool's prefill by location and both trace paths: 55 countries
    change from open dump to sanitary landfill, among them Turkey, Saudi Arabia,
    Kazakhstan, Mexico, Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Australia and the high-income
    Caribbean islands. None changes the other way. A site there models at MCF
    1.0 with oxidation 0.10 instead of 0.6 with none, about 1.5 times the methane.
  - A listed site with no type in the site tool's prefill follows the rule too;
    it was always an open dump.
  - `site_only_estimate`: 66 countries change, the 55 plus Eastern Europe.
  - The city table's last fallback (no landfill data, and no default split for
    the country or the region): only Brunei changes.
  - Climate TRACE's pipeline does not move. It already used this rule.
  - `dst_baseline_blank` is untouched: #74 rewrites its fallback.
  ([#83](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/83))

- **Gas collection efficiency and oxidation follow a site's reports year by year
  when its rows carry their year.** On rows with a `row_year` column -- which
  Climate TRACE's pipeline adds on its consolidated facility input, one row per
  site per year -- `_resolve_gas_capture` and `_build_oxidation_series` read each
  value as belonging to its row's year. The new `_carried_series` makes one value
  the value in every year, and carries several back and forward: the first back to
  the start, each forward to the next report, the latest to the end.
  - A modelled site, which has no reported-emissions year, now keeps its measured
    gas collection efficiency instead of the site-type default.
  - Rows without `row_year` keep the old rule: the site's mean, overwritten in
    reported years.
  - No number moves on today's data: every Climate TRACE site with a measured
    efficiency also reports methane, and the consolidated table gives one value
    per site.
  ([#84](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/84))

## Fixed

- **Indonesia has a per-capita waste rate of its own** (model-output change).
  `defaults_2019.msw_per_capita_country` copies IPCC 2019 Refinement, Vol. 5
  Ch. 2, Table 2A.1 (Updated), 2010 column, in tonnes per person per year. All
  167 of its rows match the IPCC's, but Indonesia's, 0.19, was never typed in.
  Indonesia fell back to the South-Eastern Asia default, 0.46. The IPCC's source
  for the row, What a Waste 2012, agrees: 0.52 kg per person per day is 0.19 t
  a year.
  - A Custom Location (`dst_baseline_blank`) in Indonesia is sized at 190,000 t
    a year per million people, down from 460,000 (−58.7%). Its emissions fall by
    the same share once a Custom Location in South-Eastern Asia disposes of its
    waste ([#74](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/74)); until then
    they are zero either way.
  - WasteMAP's June 2026 cities table does not move. All 11 Indonesian cities
    have waste figures of their own, so `load_andre_params` never reaches the
    default for them.
  - Climate TRACE's pipeline does not move. It drops a site with no tonnage
    before SWEET runs.

  The comments on `msw_per_capita_defaults` and `msw_per_capita_country` now
  name the IPCC tables they come from.
  ([#76](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/76))

- **`load_csv_new` normalizes the composition it reads** (model-output change).
  It was the one loader that took a city's ten `Waste Components` as published.
  `load_andre_params`, `dst_baseline_blank` and the site loaders all scale a
  composition to sum to 1. WasteMAP's cities table publishes Brazil's
  precomputed rows with their source composition as it is:
  - São Paulo: 99%
  - Brasília: 87%
  - Rio de Janeiro: 99.59%
  - The national default the other Brazilian rows carry: 100.1%

  Two things followed:
  - `implement_dst_changes_simple_v1_5` raised "Invalid waste fractions" for São
    Paulo and Brasília, whatever the levers. Its check rejects a composition more
    than 1% off, and São Paulo's fails by float rounding alone
    (1 − 0.99 = 0.010000000000000009).
  - The typed masses `load_csv_new` builds missed or exceeded the city's waste by
    the same percentage. The no-change scenario then drifted from the baseline
    after the implementation year, for 2,233 cities by up to 5.5e-4.

  Each type now keeps its published share of the reported total. Across all
  5,689 city names of WasteMAP's June 2026 table, before and after:
  - São Paulo's and Brasília's scenarios run. No other city's result changes
    status.
  - The city DST baseline and scenarios scale by 100 ÷ the published sum:
    - Brasília +14.9%
    - São Paulo +1.0%
    - Rio de Janeiro +0.4%
    - The 4,691 cities on Brazil's default −0.1%
  - The 995 cities whose composition already adds up do not move: 949 are
    bit-identical, and the rest are within 1e-15.
  - The no-change scenario reproduces the baseline for every city.

  Climate TRACE's pipeline does not call `load_csv_new`, so its outputs do not
  move.
  ([#73](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/73),
  [RMI/WasteMAP#852](https://github.com/RMI/WasteMAP/pull/852))

- **A Custom Location buries or dumps its waste in every country**
  (model-output change). `City.dst_baseline_blank`, which WasteMAP runs for any
  city not in the cities table, looked a country's disposal split up as
  `fraction_open_dumped_country.get(iso3, fraction_open_dumped.get(region, 0))`,
  and the same for `fraction_landfilled`. Two gaps in those tables made the split
  (0, 0, 0):
  - **Southern Asia, South-Eastern Asia and Southern Africa have no regional
    row.** This is not a label mismatch: every other regional table carries these
    three regions under the same names, as `0.0  # np.nan`. None of their 28
    countries here has a row of its own.
  - **Canada's, Germany's and Switzerland's country rows are all zeros.**

  The normalization step skipped a zero total, so every landfill's
  `fraction_of_waste` was 0 and the waste the city didn't divert left the model.
  The `except KeyError` fallback written for this never ran, because `.get`
  never raises.

  The new `defaults_2019.disposal_split_for(iso3, region)` uses the country's row
  when it sends waste to a landfill or a dump, else the region's. A place with
  neither is all landfill where a site of unknown type is assumed a sanitary
  landfill (`site_type_defaults.landfill_is_default`, Climate TRACE's rule), and
  all dumpsite elsewhere. That is the split `City.load_andre_params` gives a
  cities-table row with no landfill data, which a test checks for one country
  per region and rule. `load_andre_params` itself is unchanged, so the cities
  table doesn't move.

  2040 emissions for a Custom Location of 2 million people (500 mm, 25 °C):
  - **27 of the 28 countries in the three regions dump all their waste.**
    Pakistan goes from 0 to 21,156 t CH4, the Philippines to 10,513, South Africa
    to 33,478 and India to 5,284.
  - **Brunei, which is high income, landfills it**, from 0 to 24,983 t.
  - **Canada, Germany and Switzerland landfill it.** Canada goes from 40 to
    74,846 t, Germany from 26 to 29,521 and Switzerland from 36 to 17,263. The
    old figures were compost alone.
  - **The other 218 countries are bit-identical.** This was checked across all
    249 countries a Custom Location could be built for. Kosovo's `XKX` couldn't
    be looked up then; #77 fixes that.

  Tests: `tests/test_custom_location_disposal_split.py`.
  ([#74](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/74),
  [RMI/WasteMAP#853](https://github.com/RMI/WasteMAP/pull/853))

- **Moving waste to a city's existing gas capture changes its emissions**
  (model-output change). `City.implement_dst_changes_simple_v1_5` with
  `move_gas=1` and `existing_gas_pct` is the City DST's "% of waste diverted to
  existing landfill with gas capture" slider. Its generic branch rewrote
  `split_fractions`, but each landfill's waste comes from its own
  `fraction_of_waste`, and only the "new landfill with gas capture" block copied
  the split there. WasteMAP offers this slider only to a city that already has gas
  capture, and switches the new-landfill lever off for exactly those cities, so the
  two never ran together. On the June 2026 cities table, all 7 cities with a live
  slider (Mexico City, Lahore, Santiago, Amman, Tegucigalpa, Cebu City, Tunis)
  returned the no-change scenario exactly, at every setting.

  The arithmetic was wrong as well. It set the capture share to `existing_gas_pct`
  and multiplied the shares without capture by `existing / original`, scaling them
  up rather than down: Lahore's landfill without capture came out at 171% of the
  city's waste, and Mexico City's at 3,848%. A city with no capture had both shares
  zeroed. With a new landfill with gas capture as well, the inflated split failed
  its sum check, and that check built `CustomError` with one argument, so the API
  returned a 500.

  The new `_move_to_existing_gas_capture` gives the capture share
  `existing_gas_pct`. The landfill and dumpsite without capture share the rest in
  the proportions they had, and the scenario applies the split to the landfills
  from the implementation year. In 2040:
  - Moved to 90% capture: Lahore −45%, Mexico City −43%, Cebu City −30%.
  - Moved to 100%: Lahore −53%, Mexico City −48%.
  - Moving waste off capture raises emissions. Santiago (98.8% to capture) moved
    to 0% emits 96% more.

  Three requests the lever can't carry out now return `INVALID_PARAMETERS`
  instead of the no-change scenario:
  - a share outside [0, 1];
  - a share above 0 for a city with no landfill with gas capture (WasteMAP doesn't
    offer the lever there);
  - less than 100% for a city that sends all its waste to capture. WasteMAP
    disables the slider for the 41 such cities in the June table and sends 100%,
    which leaves the split as it is.

  Nothing else moves. The no-change scenario, the other levers and the 41 cities at
  100% give the same numbers as before. The sum check now raises a proper
  `INVALID_PARAMETERS` too.
  ([#75](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/75), [RMI/WasteMAP#854](https://github.com/RMI/WasteMAP/pull/854))

- **"Add gas capture" applies the oxidation it sets** (model-output change). In
  the generic branch of `City.implement_dst_changes_simple_v1_5`, `add_gas`
  converts a city's two sites without gas capture after the implementation year:
  - the landfill gets 60% gas capture and an oxidation factor of 0.22 (was 0.1)
  - the dumpsite becomes a controlled dumpsite with 30% capture and 0.1 (was 0)

  It set `skip_ox = True` so that `Landfill.estimate_emissions` would keep those
  factors. The emissions loop at the end of the method then replaced it with
  `skip_ox = scenario_parameters.sites_method`, which is false on this branch.
  So `estimate_emissions` reset each factor from the site's `gas_capture` flag,
  which `add_gas` doesn't set: 0.1 for the landfill and 0 for the dumpsite, in
  every year. The gas capture applied; the oxidation never did. Both lines date
  from the repository's first commit.

  0.22 and 0.1 are the values `estimate_emissions` itself gives a landfill and a
  controlled dumpsite with gas collection (its `ox_cap` table, and
  `defaults_2019.oxidation_factor["with_lfg"]`). The real-sites branch also gives
  a sanitary landfill 0.22 when it adds capture. Each branch now sets `skip_ox`
  itself, and the loop uses it. The real-sites branch keeps `skip_ox = True`.

  Measured across WasteMAP's June 2026 cities table (5,689 city names) and a
  Custom Location in each of 249 countries: gas capture added in 2026, with each
  city's own diversions.
  - **The 168 cities the UI offers the lever to** (no gas capture, some landfill)
    fall another 10.0–13.3% in 2040. Against the no-change scenario:
    - Lima and Riyadh, which landfill everything: −60.0% → −65.3%
    - Cairo, which dumps 82% of its waste: −37.3% → −43.9%
  - **A city that only dumps its waste** goes from −30.0% to −37.0%. That covers
    187 cities, among them Lagos, Karachi, Dhaka and Delhi. The UI doesn't offer
    them the lever, but the API runs it.
  - **Custom Locations** move the same way in the 218 countries where they
    landfill or dump. The other 31 have the empty disposal split that
    [#74](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/74) fixes.
  - **Brazil's 4,694 precomputed cities don't move yet.** WasteMAP's `load_city`
    sends them down the real-sites branch, where `add_gas` changes nothing for
    generic sites. Once [RMI/WasteMAP#851](https://github.com/RMI/WasteMAP/pull/851)
    sends them down the generic branch, they fall another 10.0–13.3% as well.
  - **Nothing else moves.** The no-change scenario is bit-identical for every
    city and Custom Location, and so is every `add_gas` result up to and
    including the implementation year. Without `add_gas`, `skip_ox` is what it
    was, so no other lever changes. No scenario that ran before fails now, or the
    reverse.

  Climate TRACE's pipeline doesn't call this method, so its outputs do not move.

  Tests: `tests/test_add_gas_capture_oxidation.py`.
  ([#78](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/78),
  [RMI/WasteMAP#856](https://github.com/RMI/WasteMAP/pull/856))

- **A Custom Location or site in Kosovo is modelled as Kosovo** (model-output
  change for a country given by name). `_iso3_for`, which `dst_baseline_blank`,
  `cityparams_obj_for_blank_site` and `sdst_prepopulate` use to turn the caller's
  country into a code, asked pycountry first. pycountry has no Kosovo:
  - **By code it raised.** XKX is the code SWEET's defaults and WasteMAP's
    country list key Kosovo by. pycountry raised `LookupError` for it, so
    `dst_baseline_blank` and `cityparams_obj_for_blank_site` raised
    `ValueError("Country 'XKX' not found.")`. WasteMAP's city tool answered 500
    for a Custom Location in Kosovo, and its site tool did the same for a
    Climate TRACE site there.
  - **By name it was Serbia.** The fuzzy search resolved "Kosovo" to Serbia,
    whose province Kosovo-Metohija matches. `sdst_prepopulate` names the country
    with the geocoder's English name, so the site tool's prefill for a point in
    Kosovo returned Serbia's code and defaults, and the run that followed
    modelled Serbia. The same search resolved "Curacao" to the Netherlands, and
    found nothing for Macau, Pitcairn Islands, Svalbard and Jan Mayen Islands,
    US Minor Islands, or French Southern and Antarctic Lands.

  `_iso3_for` now tries SWEET's own table first, by code
  (`defaults_2019.region_lookup_iso3`) and then by name
  (`defaults_2019.country_to_iso3`), ignoring case as pycountry does. Anything
  else goes to pycountry as before. Kosovo has no country rows in SWEET's
  defaults, so it takes Southern Europe's, and it grows with its own UN
  population series.

  Effect:
  - **A Custom Location in Kosovo** of 2 million people (500 mm, 25 °C) emits
    38,768 t CH4 in 2040. By code it raised before; by name it was Serbia's
    26,818 t.
  - **The other 249 countries WasteMAP offers are bit-identical.** Each code
    already resolved to itself.
  - **The site tool's prefill for a point in Kosovo** returns XKX instead of
    SRB. With it come Southern Europe's composition (food 35.7% instead of
    Serbia's 44.3%), a food k of 0.333 instead of 0.143, and Kosovo's population
    growth (−0.08% a year instead of −0.76%).
  - **Climate TRACE's pipeline is unaffected.** None of its paths calls
    `_iso3_for`.

  Tests: `tests/test_country_lookup.py`.
  ([#77](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/77),
  [RMI/WasteMAP#857](https://github.com/RMI/WasteMAP/pull/857))

- **A blank or junk country is not found, instead of being modelled as another
  country; the site tool's prefill finds every country the geocoder names**
  (model-output change for the prefill in Sint Maarten). `_iso3_for` ends with
  pycountry's fuzzy search, which scores every country for an empty name and
  scores a country for any of its subdivisions that match:
  - **No country was the United Kingdom.** `""` and `" "` resolved to GBR, the
    country with the most subdivisions, so a Custom Location or site sent
    `country=` was modelled as the UK (30,434 t CH4 in 2040 for 2 million
    people). `None` raised `AttributeError`, not `LookupError`, so the callers
    answered with a crash rather than "not found".
  - **A province's name was its country.** "None" was Italy (Frosinone), "nan"
    Thailand (Nan province), "-" France, "0" Sweden, "Sint Maarten" the
    Netherlands rather than SXM.
  - **The prefill crashed for a point in no country.** Nominatim can't place a
    point at sea, so geopy returns None, and its address for Antarctica has no
    country. Both raised `AttributeError`.
  - **The prefill failed for 11 names Nominatim uses.** Measured over 306
    reverse-geocoded points (one per country and territory): "Turkey", "Ivory
    Coast", "Cape Verde", "East Timor", "Democratic Republic of the Congo",
    "Congo-Brazzaville", "Palestinian Territories", "Sahrawi Arab Democratic
    Republic", "Northern Cyprus", "Somaliland" and "South Ossetia" are spelled
    neither SWEET's nor pycountry's way, so each raised "Country '...' not
    found."

  `_iso3_for` now raises `LookupError` for anything that isn't a non-blank
  string, strips the name, and takes the fuzzy search's answer only when the
  name is whole words of that country's own name, or its initials ("UK",
  "UAE"). A province's name is no longer found even where the answer was right
  ("England"). The prefill (`_iso3_at`) tries the geocoder's English name, then
  its ISO 3166-1 code, and says "No country at <lat>, <lon>." where there is
  none. The name comes first because SWEET has its own codes for Aruba, Curaçao
  and Sint Maarten, which Nominatim codes as the Netherlands.

  Effect:
  - **Over the 222 country names Nominatim gave**, 210 resolve as before (the
    breakaway regions it names as countries resolve through its code to the
    country they're in: Abkhazia and South Ossetia to Georgia, Northern Cyprus
    to Cyprus, Somaliland to Somalia). The 11 above now resolve. Sint Maarten
    moves from NLD to SXM: an open dump rather than a landfill, food 48.2%
    rather than 35.0%, growth 0.63% a year rather than 0.15%.
  - **Every code and name in SWEET's own table resolves as before**, so the
    city tool's Custom Location, which sends a code, is bit-identical for every
    country.
  - **Climate TRACE's pipeline is unaffected.** None of its paths calls
    `_iso3_for`.

  Tests: `tests/test_country_lookup.py`.
  ([#80](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/80),
  [RMI/WasteMAP#859](https://github.com/RMI/WasteMAP/pull/859))

- **A city with no waste figure generates waste at the default rate**
  (model-output change). When a cities-table row has neither
  `msw_generated_metric_tons_per_year` nor `msw_collected_metric_tons_per_year`,
  `City.load_andre_params` sizes the city's waste as the IPCC per-capita default
  (`defaults_2019.msw_per_capita_country`, else the region's
  `msw_per_capita_defaults`) times the city's population. That population is
  the one for the row's `population_year`, so the mass already describes that
  year. The loader labelled it 2019 instead, and the adjustment that moves a
  surveyed tonnage from its survey year to the population year then moved it a
  second time. The city got the default × P(population year) / P(2019), or the
  compounded equivalent for a big city on its own UN rates. The default now
  takes the population year as its year, so that adjustment leaves it alone. A
  surveyed tonnage is still moved as before.
  - **Who moves:** the 39 of 5,962 cities in WasteMAP's June 2026 cities table
    with "Defaults Used: Waste Mass". Their waste, and so their CH4, changes by
    −7.9% (Abeokuta, population year 2022) to +53.9% (Kano, 2006): 30 rise and
    9 fall. Kuwait City goes from 7.92 to 8.36 kg per person per day (+5.5%),
    Tel Aviv +14.7%, Melbourne +5.4%. Their 2025 total, 395 kt CH4 as
    published, rises by about 27 kt.
  - **What else changes for those rows:** "Year of Data Collection (MSW)"
    becomes the population year (2005–2022) instead of 2019 (Hugh's call,
    2026-10-17). WasteMAP's map build appends that year to the three "Data
    Source" columns, so those follow. WasteMAP's API already returns the
    population year as `waste_mass_year` (RMI/WasteMAP#860), so the label and
    that field now agree.
  - **When:** nothing moves until WasteMAP's cities table is rebuilt, because
    the map and the City DST read the published table. Climate TRACE doesn't
    call `load_andre_params`.
  - `City.import_basics`, the SINIR loader, had the same rule and gets the same
    fix. Only `model_city_via_sites` reaches it, and nothing calls that.
  - The copies in `import_basics_site` are unchanged. They size a site for a
    placeholder population of 100, so a default there means nothing either
    way, and Climate TRACE drops a site with no tonnage before SWEET runs.

  Tests: `tests/test_default_waste_per_capita.py`.
  ([#79](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/79))

## Known limitations

- **The three regions fall back to all dumpsite, Brunei apart.** Central Asia
  and Rest of Oceania have done the same since #18, for the same reason: the
  source gives them no disposal figures. That is coarse for countries such as South Africa,
  which landfill most of their waste. The cities table uses the same fallback.
  ([#74](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/74))

- **Germany and Switzerland now landfill everything they don't compost or
  burn.** Their rows file the rest under "unspecified", which holds recycling,
  and Custom Location has no recycling default. That overstates their
  landfilling, as it already did for Japan, Austria, the Netherlands and Sweden,
  whose rows landfill 1–3%. ([#74](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/74))

- **Sri Lanka gives the highest figure of the 31: 122,745 t.** Its per-capita
  default in `msw_per_capita_country` is 1.86 t/year (5.1 kg/person/day).
  Fourteen countries there are above 3 kg/person/day, Kuwait highest at 8.4.
  That is a separate data problem, which the zero split had hidden for Sri Lanka.
  ([#74](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/74))
