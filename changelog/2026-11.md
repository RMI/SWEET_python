# SWEET_python Changelog — November 2026

**Highlights:** The city model has a controlled dumpsite. A city's
controlled-dumpsite share, which the model used to drop, now goes to a fourth
disposal bucket with a dump's MCF and a controlled dump's oxidation (no output
change until a caller passes the share). In the city DST, a scenario that changes
nothing now equals the baseline, more diversion no longer sends more paper to the
landfill, and incineration burns what composting and recycling leave.

## Added

- **The city model sends a controlled-dumpsite share to a controlled dumpsite**
  (no output change until a caller passes the share,
  [#121](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/121)). The generic city model had
  three disposal buckets: a landfill with gas capture, one without, and a dumpsite.
  A controlled-dumpsite share, such as the "aterro controlado" share the SINIR
  loader puts in WasteMAP's `city_waste.waste_treatment_controlled_dumpsite_percent`,
  had nowhere to go: `load_andre_params` never read it, and the split was rescaled
  so the other shares filled its place. A city sending 40% to controlled dumps and
  60% to open dumps was modelled as 100% open dump.
  - `SplitFractions` has a `controlled_dumpsite` share, 0 unless given.
    `load_andre_params` reads `waste_treatment_controlled_dumpsite_percent`, and
    `load_csv_new` and `load_from_csv` read `Percent of Waste to Controlled
    Dumpsites (%)`; a row or table without the column sends it nothing. The share
    counts toward the disposal a city reports, so a city whose reported disposal
    adds up to 100% only with it no longer takes its country's default diversion.
  - The city's fourth landfill takes it: the uncategorised MCF (0.6, the same as
    an open dump's) and oxidation 0.05 without gas capture. It is last, at
    `CONTROLLED_DUMPSITE_LANDFILL` (3), so the first three keep their places.
  - The City DST's levers treat it as a site without capture. "Add gas capture"
    gives it a controlled dump's capture (45%) and oxidation with capture (0.10)
    from the year after implementation; it stays a controlled dumpsite, so its MCF
    does not move. "Move waste to existing gas capture" and a new landfill with
    gas capture scale it with the other shares. The new landfill comes after it,
    and a landfill the baseline does not have is the one that takes no waste before
    implementation, where before it was whichever sat at index 3.
  - Checked against `main` for seven cities with no controlled-dumpsite share
    (four cities-table rows, three Custom Locations), baseline and every DST
    lever: identical emissions.

## Fixed

- **A city that incinerates all of a waste type keeps its landfills** (output
  change for those cities only, [#124](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/124)).
  When diversion takes all of a type, generated minus diverted comes out a few
  nanotonnes below zero: Osaka's paper at −6e-11 t, Yokohama's food at −9e-5 t.
  `mass_checker_math` accepts that, since it allows diversion to exceed a type's
  share by up to 1e-5 of the city's waste. But `_calculate_net_masses` kept the
  negative, so `load_andre_params` printed "Invalid new value" and returned with no
  landfills and no `input_problems`, and WasteMAP's cities build stopped on Osaka.
  - The two steps now share one tolerance, `MASS_CHECK_TOLERANCE` (1e-5).
    `_calculate_net_masses` sets a leftover within it to 0 in all three of its
    paths, each year judged against that year's waste. A larger deficit stays
    negative and is still refused.
  - Osaka and Yokohama each model four landfills again. This does not touch the
    400 that Yokohama's and Naha's city DST scenarios answer ("Negative mass for
    green (food) in scenario 1"): that comes from a separate strict check in
    `implement_dst_changes_simple_v1_5`.
  - Tests: `tests/test_net_masses_rounding.py`, with the two cities' rows from
    WasteMAP's cities table as a fixture.
- **A city DST scenario that changes nothing equals the baseline, more diversion
  never landfills more of any waste type, and incineration burns what composting,
  digestion and recycling leave** (output change for city DST scenarios, Custom
  Locations, and the baselines of 59 published cities, [#127](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/127)).
  An end-to-end check of WasteMAP's cities on 2026-11-10 found two bugs, in
  `sweet/2026.11.2` as on `main`.
  - `load_csv_new` and `load_from_csv` read the metal, glass and other shares of a
    city's incinerated waste as 0, though incineration takes every type. So any
    scenario with incineration fell short of its incineration share and went to the
    code that adjusts the split.
  - When the diversions together asked for more of a waste type than the city has,
    that code gave the type to the diversions fewest others share: composting took
    all the food and green waste first, recycling took plastic, metal and glass
    first, and paper was landfilled. Only that code burned what the others left; a
    split that fit burned in proportion to all of the city's waste. So a scenario
    jumped as a slider crossed from one to the other.
  - Now composting, digestion and recycling each keep their own mix, the shares of
    each type they take. A type they ask for beyond what the city has is shared
    among them, and each makes up the rest from its other types in its own
    proportions (`dst_allocation.proportional_allocation`).
  - Incineration burns what those three leave, the same share of every type left
    (`dst_allocation.incineration_mix`), in the baseline and every scenario. An
    incinerator takes the waste left after separate collection, so when composting
    grows, the incinerator and the landfill each give up food in proportion. The
    loaders work this mix out from the published composting, digestion and
    recycling instead of reading the table's incineration columns, so a DST
    baseline and its no-change scenario agree whatever table they come from.
    Hugh's choice, 2026-11-10; `diversion-split` in `MODEL_DECISIONS.md`.
  - 2040 methane against the baseline, on the review's cities table (WasteMAP #969
    with SWEET #126):

    | City | Scenario | Before | After |
    |---|---|---:|---:|
    | London | no change | +19.1% | 0.0% |
    | Bristol | no change | +7.3% | 0.0% |
    | Seattle | incineration +5 points | +14.4% | −5.8% |
    | Navi Mumbai | recycling +5 points | +24.3% | −20.7% |

  - On WasteMAP's cities table of 2026-11-10 (all 399 cities outside Brazil and
    300 of Brazil's), 39 no-change scenarios missed their baseline, by up to +53%
    (Eindhoven), and 5 more points of incineration raised 2040 methane in 110
    cities, by up to +31%. Now every no-change scenario equals its baseline (to
    rounding for Naha and Yokohama, which divert all their waste and make almost
    no methane), and 5 more points of any diversion lowers every city's methane in
    every year after implementation.
  - The cities build uses the same rules, so 59 published cities' baselines move
    when WasteMAP rebuilds with this release. Their total barely moves: the
    published cities' 2025 methane falls 0.09%.
    - Composting with recycling falls, since composting no longer leaves paper to
      the landfill: Seoul −48%, Bangkok −46%, Madrid −26%, six Indian cities −4% to
      −43%.
    - Incineration with recycling mostly rises: London +9%, Vienna +28%, Hamburg
      +17%, Zurich +34%, Stockholm +27%, Oslo +57%. Recycling takes paper, so what is
      left, and burned or landfilled alike, holds less food and more paper than the
      whole mix, and paper makes more methane per tonne.
    - Five Japanese cities that incinerate or recycle nearly all their waste rise:
      Yokohama 0 to 3,876 t, Kitakyushu 67 to 1,396 t, Kobe 707 to 1,868 t, Toyama 4
      to 581 t, Naha 0 to 220 t. Their recycling now takes paper, and SWEET
      landfills 22.5% of recycled paper as rejects (`diversion-rejects`).
  - Raising composting can still raise methane slightly when a city diverts nearly
    all its waste: more composting then takes some food the incinerator would have
    burned, and landfills composting's rejects. Bristol at 27% to 29% composting,
    with 99% of its waste diverted: +0.02% in 2040 per point.
  - A table built with the old split can publish a mix that leaves out a type the
    diversion takes: Coimbatore's composting is all food. Such a type gets a
    weight of one in a million, so the diversion takes it only once the named
    types run out, and the slider reaches the same maximum as before.
  - Diverting exactly all of a city's waste still fits when rounding leaves it a
    billionth short (Kitakyushu).
  - A scenario's fill-in for a diversion with no published mix gave the types it
    can't take a share too; now it gives them none. No number changes.
  - Climate TRACE's site paths don't use this split, so its numbers don't move.
  - Tests: `tests/test_city_dst_scenarios_match_the_baseline.py`, with London,
    Bristol, Seattle and Navi Mumbai as the review's table published them, and the
    split's properties in `tests/test_dst_allocation.py`.
