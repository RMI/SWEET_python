# SWEET_python Changelog — November 2026

**Highlights:** The city model has a controlled dumpsite. A city's
controlled-dumpsite share, which the model used to drop, now goes to a fourth
disposal bucket with a dump's MCF and a controlled dump's oxidation (no output
change until a caller passes the share).

## Added

- **The city model sends a controlled-dumpsite share to a controlled dumpsite**
  (no output change until a caller passes the share,
  [#121](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/121)). The generic city model had
  three disposal buckets: a landfill with gas capture, one without, and a dumpsite.
  A controlled-dumpsite share, such as the "aterro controlado" share the SINIR
  loader puts in WasteMAP's `city_waste.waste_treatment_controlled_dumpsite_percent`,
  had nowhere to go: `load_andre_params` never read it, and the split was rescaled
  so the other shares filled its place. A city sending 40% to controlled dumps and
  60% to open dumps was modelled as 100% open dump.
  - `SplitFractions` has a `controlled_dumpsite` share, 0 unless given.
    `load_andre_params` reads `waste_treatment_controlled_dumpsite_percent`, and
    `load_csv_new` and `load_from_csv` read `Percent of Waste to Controlled
    Dumpsites (%)`; a row or table without the column sends it nothing. The share
    counts toward the disposal a city reports, so a city whose reported disposal
    adds up to 100% only with it no longer takes its country's default diversion.
  - The city's fourth landfill takes it: the uncategorised MCF (0.6, the same as
    an open dump's) and oxidation 0.05 without gas capture. It is last, at
    `CONTROLLED_DUMPSITE_LANDFILL` (3), so the first three keep their places.
  - The City DST's levers treat it as a site without capture. "Add gas capture"
    gives it a controlled dump's capture (45%) and oxidation with capture (0.10)
    from the year after implementation; it stays a controlled dumpsite, so its MCF
    does not move. "Move waste to existing gas capture" and a new landfill with
    gas capture scale it with the other shares. The new landfill comes after it,
    and a landfill the baseline does not have is the one that takes no waste before
    implementation, where before it was whichever sat at index 3.
  - Checked against `main` for seven cities with no controlled-dumpsite share
    (four cities-table rows, three Custom Locations), baseline and every DST
    lever: identical emissions.
- **`defaults_2019` keeps generated and disposed country mixes apart** (no output
  change: nothing reads them yet,
  [#128](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/128)). A country mix describes either all the
  municipal waste a country *generates*, before recycling, composting or burning,
  or the waste *disposed* of at landfills and dumps. The city model starts from
  generated tonnes and takes diversion out itself; a Climate TRACE site, the site
  tool and a national landfill total start from tonnes disposed. Both read
  `WASTE_FRACTIONS_COUNTRY`, IPCC 2019 Table 2A.2, whose rows are of both kinds
  whatever their label. Checked against the sources they cite, 12 describe waste
  sent to landfill and 6 waste generated, and 7 cite a source that does not hold
  their numbers. Most of the rest look like surveys of collected waste, often one
  city's, but were not checked.
  - `WASTE_FRACTIONS_GENERATED_COUNTRY`: the 28 EU countries of 2015, from the
    European Reference Model on Municipal Waste (2018 update, Annex 2, Appendix 1),
    kept as printed in `ERM_2015_COMPOSITION` and mapped onto the ten waste types by
    `ERM_TO_WASTE_TYPE`. Its "other biowaste" goes to green, as the IPCC groups
    other organic putrescible waste with garden waste. Ghana and Zimbabwe are added
    from the IPCC table, whose rows for them their sources describe as generated,
    and seven countries from national sources, each checked against its
    publication: Brazil (Abrelpe 2020), Switzerland (BAFU 2022), Algeria (2018/19
    campaign), Hong Kong (EPD 2022), Peru (SIGERSOL 2023), the US (EPA 2018) and
    Zambia (2021 household sampling).
  - `WASTE_FRACTIONS_DISPOSED_COUNTRY`: the 10 rows of the IPCC table whose cited
    source describes waste sent to landfill (Croatia, Czechia, France, Iceland,
    Ireland, Luxembourg, Poland, Switzerland, the UK and the US), read from that
    table rather than copied, plus Austria (NID 2025, residual waste landfilled in
    2008), Spain (NID 2026, mixed waste landfilled 2012–2024) and Italy (IPCC's
    corrected row, from its 2018 inventory), and seven countries from national
    sources: Brazil (collected waste, with little diverted), Chile, Hong Kong,
    Latvia (its inventory read again), New Zealand (MfE 2020), Panama and Trinidad
    and Tobago. Five rows taken from inventories are
    generated mixes by their own inventory's account (Bulgaria, Moldova, Russia,
    Sweden, Zimbabwe), and Germany's and the Netherlands' numbers are not in the
    inventories IPCC cites; none of them is in this table. IPCC's Latvia row put
    the inventory's 47.9% organic waste into "other".
  - A new `docs/` folder holds `WASTE_COMPOSITION_DEFAULTS.md`: every row's source,
    the rows left out and why, and what each IPCC country row describes.
  - `WASTE_FRACTIONS_GENERATED_SOURCES` and `WASTE_FRACTIONS_DISPOSED_SOURCES` name
    each row's source, and `waste_composition_by_basis(iso3, basis)` returns a row
    as fractions, or None when the country has none.
  - `waste_composition_for()` is unchanged, so every path takes the same default.
    Found on the way: SWEET's composition tables are IPCC's November 2019 printing.
    IPCC reissued the chapter at the same address in July 2023, correcting Italy's
    row (food 12.6 → 21.9, paper 39.2 → 23.9, plastic 27.6 → 11.8) and so the
    Southern Europe average. `WASTE_FRACTIONS_COUNTRY` and the regional defaults
    keep the 2019 printing for now; the disposed table's Italy row is the July
    2023 one.

- **A composition surveyed after diversion can have the diversion added back**
  (no output change until a caller passes it,
  [#126](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/126)). `load_andre_params(...,
  composition_after_diversion=True)` reads the row's composition as the waste left
  after diversion, what reached the landfill or dump, and adds back what each
  pathway took before modelling the city. Without it, SWEET took the diverted
  materials out a second time. Seattle recycles 58.0% of its waste and 46.1% of its
  surveyed composition is recyclable, so loading it raised "Recycling can be at
  most 46.1% of this city's waste".
  - `after_diversion.composition_before_diversion` undoes SWEET's own diversion,
    which takes each pathway's share from the types it can take, in proportion to
    the generated mix. Diverting from its answer leaves the surveyed composition
    exactly. Seattle's paper goes from 20.3% to 34.1%, and its food from 53.8% to
    22.6%.
  - SWEET still landfills each pathway's rejects on top (`diversion-rejects`), as for
    every city: the recycling and compost residue a survey of garbage trucks doesn't
    see. So Seattle's landfill gets 51% of its waste, against the survey's 42%, and
    its landfilled mix is 44% food, not the survey's 54%.
  - The tonnage and the compost emission factor are not touched: the factor depends
    only on food against green, which the add-back keeps. A row with no usable
    composition is refused, as is diversion of 100% or within 1e-9 of it (scaled
    shares can add up to 0.9999999999999999), or a pathway none of whose types is left
    in the survey.
  - Tests: `tests/test_composition_after_diversion.py`, with six cities' rows from
    WasteMAP's cities table as a fixture: Seattle, Canberra, Bristol, Tadipatri,
    Itanagar and Navi Mumbai. All six report diverting more than their mix holds.
    WasteMAP reads only Seattle's and Bristol's as surveyed after diversion.

## Fixed

- **A city that incinerates all of a waste type keeps its landfills** (output
  change for those cities only, [#124](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/124)).
  When diversion takes all of a type, generated minus diverted comes out a few
  nanotonnes below zero: Osaka's paper at −6e-11 t, Yokohama's food at −9e-5 t.
  `mass_checker_math` accepts that, since it allows diversion to exceed a type's
  share by up to 1e-5 of the city's waste. But `_calculate_net_masses` kept the
  negative, so `load_andre_params` printed "Invalid new value" and returned with no
  landfills and no `input_problems`, and WasteMAP's cities build stopped on
  Yokohama (Osaka is on WasteMAP's skip list, so the build never reached it).
  - The two steps now share one tolerance, `MASS_CHECK_TOLERANCE` (1e-5).
    `_calculate_net_masses` sets a leftover within it to 0 in all three of its
    paths, each year judged against that year's waste. A larger deficit stays
    negative and is still refused.
  - Osaka and Yokohama each model four landfills again. This does not touch the
    400 that Yokohama's and Naha's city DST scenarios answer ("Negative mass for
    green (food) in scenario 1"): that comes from a separate strict check in
    `implement_dst_changes_simple_v1_5`.
  - Tests: `tests/test_net_masses_rounding.py`, with the two cities' rows from
    WasteMAP's cities table as a fixture.
