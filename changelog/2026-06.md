# SWEET_python Changelog — June 2026

**Highlights:** This month added the advanced DST (ADST) modeling path — a single-site module and its city-level, multi-landfill sibling — that lets callers supply waste mass and composition as time series and reuse the tested SWEET emission engine without waste generation or TRACE reconciliation. It also reworked the city DST diversion allocator onto an exact min-cost max-flow solver, kept the decomposition rate k physically defensible for cold/dry sites, centralized waste-composition defaults, and fixed a food-waste-prevention bug that produced spurious negative mass.

## Added
- Single-site advanced DST modeling module (`advanced_dst.py`, `run_advanced_dst` + `AdvancedDSTRequest`), backing the `/v1/site_emissions/adst` endpoint. The caller supplies waste mass and composition directly as time series, so the module does no waste generation, population growth, or TRACE reconciliation — it builds a throwaway City/Landfill internally purely to reuse the tested emission engine and returns baseline and scenario emission series as a pure function (WP-186). ([#23](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/23))
- City-level advanced DST modeling module (`advanced_dst_city.py`, `run_advanced_dst_city`) backing `/v1/site_emissions/adst_city_level`: models a whole city at once — diversion (compost/anaerobic/combustion/recycling) plus an arbitrary number of landfills sharing the city's net-of-diversion waste via a per-year landfill split timeline. Diversion math, reject rates, and emissions aggregation mirror the tested `City` machinery (WP-186). ([#23](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/23))
- Shared DST helper module (`dst_common.py`) for validation, time-series builders, k/MCF/oxidation, and landfill construction; the single-site ADST module was refactored onto it with behavior unchanged (WP-186). ([#23](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/23))
- `dst_allocation.py`: an exact, dependency-free (no scipy/networkx) min-cost max-flow allocator that splits the city DST diversion sliders (compost/anaerobic/recycling) across eligible waste types, with a contention-aware cost that spares combustible mass for the combustion remainder step. Includes a standalone validation harness/oracle (`dst_allocation_prototype.py`) and regression tests (`tests/test_dst_allocation.py`, `tests/test_mass_checker_integration.py`). ([#25](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/25))

## Changed
- City DST diversion allocation now routes through the new min-cost max-flow solver instead of the hand-rolled iterative redistribution in `City.mass_checker_math`. The old local-move heuristic falsely rejected many feasible slider combinations (and mishandled or crashed on others); the new approach decides feasibility correctly across the entire true feasible region while keeping the split sensible. Net effect in `city_params.py` is a large simplification (about 250 fewer lines). ([#25](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/25))
- Floored the decomposition rate k at the IPCC dry-boreal low-end default of 0.04/yr and clamped the temperature factor's input to the fitted Ratkowsky domain (equivalent to clamping ambient temperature to roughly [-10 °C, 45 °C]). Previously the Wang et al. temperature factor extrapolated outside its valid range, driving k toward zero — and even spuriously increasing — for cold/dry sites, producing implausible near-zero methane generation that was hypersensitive to tiny temperature differences. ([#24](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/24))
- Centralized default waste-composition selection into `defaults_2019.waste_composition_for(iso3, region)`, replacing the country-vs-region default branching that was duplicated (and broken — see Fixed) across many `City` methods. The helper prefers the country-specific table but falls back to the regional default when the country row is missing or has a missing/zero plastic share (the tell-tale of an incomplete row that would otherwise model an implausible no-plastic stream), and normalizes the result to sum to 1 so the percent-scale country table and fraction-scale regional table are interchangeable for callers. Also renamed the country table's `plastics` column to `plastic` to match the regional defaults and the model components. ([#24](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/24))

## Fixed
- **Country-specific waste compositions were never being applied (model-output change).** The country-vs-region selection tested `iso3 in defaults_2019.waste_fractions_country`, which checks the DataFrame's *column* labels while ISO3 codes are its *index* — so it always evaluated False, and every modeled site silently used its **regional** default composition even when a country-specific row existed. Waste composition sets the first-order-decay rate `k` (via the Wang et al. lookup), so this is not cosmetic: any site whose country composition differs from its regional default now gets a different `k` and different emissions (e.g. Indonesia's more-organic country profile roughly doubles `k` versus the South-Eastern Asia regional default). Fixed while centralizing selection into `waste_composition_for` (see Changed). ([#24](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/24))
- Preserved a missing (NaN) site temperature through the cold-weather k calculation by switching the temperature-factor clamp from `max`/`min` to `np.clip`, which keeps NaN instead of mapping it to `tmax` → tf=0 → k silently floored to 0.04. `_singapore_k` now logs which asset is missing a temperature. ([#24](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/24))
- Fixed food-waste-prevention renormalization in `City.implement_dst_changes_simple_v1_5` that produced spurious negative mass. Non-food fractions were being rescaled by `old_nonfood_total / new_nonfood_total` instead of by the actual total-reduction factor, over-inflating every non-food share so the diversion allocator believed more metal/glass/other/textiles existed than the unchanged masses held — raising a spurious "Negative mass for <type>" and erroring the city DST chart (e.g. Algiers with composting + recycling + food prevention). Now every fraction is rescaled by `(1 - food_waste_prevention * food_fraction)`, the same factor waste_mass is reduced by, preserving the invariant `waste_fractions[w] * waste_mass == waste_masses[w]`. Adds a DB-free regression suite (`tests/test_food_waste_prevention.py`). ([#25](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/25))
