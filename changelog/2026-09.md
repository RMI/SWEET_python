# SWEET_python Changelog — September 2026

**Highlights:** The annual model was charging a flare's inefficiency twice — a
hardcoded 2% slip stacked on top of a `flaring` destruction efficiency that
already accounted for it — which also made the annual engine disagree with the
monthly one by ~3.7% on identical inputs. A site with gas capture now emits
about 3.5% less; sites without capture are unaffected. Separately,
`gas_capture_efficiency` is now bounded to [0, 1] on both advanced-DST paths,
where an out-of-range value used to produce negative emissions silently. The
city-level advanced DST also gains a `food_waste_prevention` input, so callers
stop having to pre-shrink the waste stream themselves, and can now ask it where
the tonnage went — the diversion split per waste type, and the total each
landfill received — instead of re-deriving it.

## Added

- **The city-level advanced DST can report its mass flow.**
  `run_advanced_dst_city(request, with_mass_flow=True)` adds a `mass_flow` key
  describing where the tonnage went, per variant and per year: `generated` (per
  waste type, before food waste prevention), `prevented`, `diverted` (per
  pathway per waste type, net of reject rates), `landfilled` (the residual), and
  `sites` (per landfill, in request order, windowed by open/close years). These
  are the frames the emissions were computed from — every one was already built
  and all but the residual's sign check discarded — so a caller rendering this
  is showing the model rather than a parallel estimate of it. That matters most
  for `diverted`: compost and anaerobic digestion draw only on organics and
  recycling only on recyclables, each with its own per-component reject rate, so
  the split is per waste type and cannot be approximated as one scalar on the
  total without composting metal and glass. A pathway the request never uses is
  omitted rather than sent as zeros; a missing pathway reads as zero. The new
  argument is keyword-only and defaults to off, so existing calls are
  unaffected and the default return is unchanged — but the signature itself did
  gain a parameter, which a caller comparing versions will see.
  ([#55](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/55))

- **`food_waste_prevention` on the city-level advanced DST.** A
  `{year: fraction}` series on `AdvancedDSTCityRequest` that removes a share of
  the city's generated food waste before anything else in the model reads the
  stream: food mass falls by that share, no other material's tonnage moves, and
  the total shrinks by exactly the food removed, with the composition
  renormalized around the smaller stream. Same lever as the city DST's
  `food_waste_prevention` (`City.implement_dst_changes_simple_v1_5`). It is
  generation-side, so it lands upstream of diversion — each pathway then takes
  its share of the smaller stream, and a pathway left without the organics to
  meet its target raises `over_diversion` rather than modelling negative
  landfilled mass. Values outside [0, 1] are clamped, matching how this module
  already treats `gas_capture_efficiency`.

  The reason it belongs here rather than in the caller: prevention is two
  coordinated edits — shrink the total, reshape the fractions — and they only
  stay in step if both are derived from *one* composition. A caller holding both
  a city composition and a site composition can pair a total scaled on one with
  fractions taken from the other, which moves the non-food masses and invents
  tonnage out of nothing. WasteMAP's ADST did exactly that. The model reads a
  single composition per request, so the two cannot disagree; the transform runs
  in mass space (multiply out, remove the food, divide back) so that the pairing
  is not expressible. **Model-output change** wherever the field is set: the
  prevented tonnage leaves the stream before diversion, so masses and the
  emissions derived from them both move. **No output change** for any request
  that omits it, which returns the inputs untouched.
  ([#54](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/54))

## Added

- **The city-level advanced DST can model an incineration facility.**
  `CityLandfillSpec.combusts` marks a facility as burning the waste routed to
  it — incineration, with or without energy recovery — rather than depositing
  it. The share of the city's disposed waste sent there still arrives there,
  but only the unburnable reject (`City.combustion_reject_rate`, 10%) is
  deposited and left to decay; the rest is destroyed and, since combustion
  produces no methane in this model, emits nothing. The burnt share is not
  re-routed to another site. The remaining per-site fields then describe the
  residue: `landfill_type` is the residue's disposal category, and the
  gas/biocover fields are the residue pile's.

  `combusts` is a `Variant`, so a facility can start or stop burning at
  `implement_year` — which is what a caller needs to model converting a dump
  into an incinerator, or retiring one. It is optional and defaults to a
  depositing site, so an existing caller is byte-identical.

  The endpoint previously had no notion of burning at all, so a caller
  describing an incinerator could only submit it as an ordinary landfill: the
  facility decayed 100% of its intake and reported methane an incinerator
  cannot physically produce. `City.sdst_v1_5` has always handled a
  waste-to-energy site correctly — combustion at 100% of intake for every open
  year, with the 10% reject landfilled — so this brings the city-level path to
  parity with the single-site one
  (`advanced_dst_city.py`, `tests/test_adst_city_incineration.py`). ([#53](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/53))

## Fixed

- **A caller can now say a landfill never closes.** `MODEL_YEAR_MAX` was the
  hard ceiling on `landfill_open_close`, but a closure year is not an intake
  year (`apply_window` zeroes from it inclusive), so a site submitted as closing
  in `MODEL_END_YEAR` accepts nothing in the model's *final* year — meaning
  `MODEL_END_YEAR` could not express "no known closure", and there was nothing
  else to send. `dst_common.NEVER_CLOSES` (`MODEL_END_YEAR + 1`) is now
  admissible and means exactly that. It is the same sentinel `city_params`
  already uses for a missing recorded close year (`fillna(MODEL_END_YEAR + 1)`),
  so the two paths now agree.

  `apply_window` is the only consumer of a close year on the advanced paths
  (`Landfill` merely stores it), and `windowed.loc[2051:]` is a no-op against an
  index ending at 2050 — so this is purely permissive. **No output change for
  any request that does not use the new value**; a request that switches to it
  gains the final year's deposits, which every ADST city had been discarding.
  The boundary itself is unchanged and now documented on `apply_window` as the
  definition, cross-referencing `city_params` and Climate TRACE's
  `time_series_ops.zero_incoming_waste_from_close_year`.
  ([#56](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/56),
  [RMI/WasteMAP#775](https://github.com/RMI/WasteMAP/pull/775))



- **The annual model charged flare inefficiency twice, and disagreed with the
  monthly model.** `ch4_capture` is already `generation × capture_efficiency ×
  flaring`, so the gas a flare fails to destroy is emitted through the
  `(ch4_produce - ch4_capture)` residual. `estimate_emissions2` added
  `+ ch4_capture * 0.02` on top of that, charging the same 2% a second time.
  The monthly path (`trace_monthly`, used by the Climate TRACE per-site runs)
  has never had the extra term, so the same inputs produced ~3.7% higher
  emissions on every DST path than on the TRACE path. The term is removed and
  the two engines now agree exactly across the full grid of capture × flaring
  combinations. **Model-output change:** a site with gas capture emits ~3.5%
  less at the default flaring of 0.98. Sites without capture are byte-for-byte
  unchanged — with `gas_capture_efficiency` at 0 the removed term was already 0.
  ([#51](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/51))

- **`gas_capture_efficiency` is bounded to [0, 1].** It is typed as a bare
  `Dict[int, float]` on both advanced-DST request models, nothing validated it,
  and the engine multiplied straight through — so `1.5` silently produced
  negative emissions and a negative value manufactured methane. Both runners now
  clip the resolved series. No output change for any in-range input.
  ([#51](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/51))

- **One methane density constant.** `landfill.py` carried both `0.7168` kg/m³
  and `0.657` for the same m³↔kg conversion — 9.1% apart, CH₄ at 0 °C versus at
  roughly 25 °C. Unified as `CH4_DENSITY_KG_PER_M3 = 0.7168`, the value the rest
  of SWEET already uses and the basis `L_0` is denominated in. No output change:
  the `0.657` sat in the `doing_fancy_ox` branch, which is unreachable because
  `doing_fancy_ox` is hardcoded `False` with its enabling logic commented out.
  ([#51](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/51))

- **A scenario opening a landfill later than the baseline lost the waste
  buried before it — in years the scenario is supposed to match the baseline
  exactly.** The city-level advanced DST runs two variants and splices them:
  the scenario's own inputs take effect at `implement_year`, and every earlier
  year is the baseline's. A landfill's open/close window is per variant,
  because a `siteClosure` intervention moves it, and two things applied the
  scenario's window to years that convention had already assigned to the
  baseline.

  `mass_scen` took the baseline's pre-implement rows and *then* had the
  scenario's window applied, so `apply_window` zeroed the mass that had just
  been spliced in. And the model derives its year range from the landfill's
  `open_date` (`model_v2.estimate_emissions2`), so starting the scenario's
  landfill at the scenario's own open year dropped those years from its
  emissions frame altogether — the two halves came back a different shape, and
  a caller subtracting them got a `KeyError` or a silently truncated diff.

  Each variant is now windowed on its own years first and the splice comes
  after, and the scenario's landfill starts at `min(baseline_open,
  scenario_open)` — the earliest year its mass frame can carry anything. The
  parameter vectors already span the wider range, which is the contract
  `estimate_emissions2` documents for its caller.

  **Modeled scenario emissions rise for any request whose scenario states a
  later opening year than its baseline**, by the decay tail of the waste those
  years bury; a site opened in 1980 and restated as 2010 was missing thirty
  years of accumulated stock. Verified on a 100,000 t/yr city: baseline's
  frame ran 1980–2050 and the scenario's 2010–2050, diverging by up to 593 on
  the fifteen pre-implement years they did share. Nothing changes when the two
  windows agree, which is the ordinary case — a request with no `siteClosure`
  and no scenario open/close of its own is byte-identical.
  ([#60](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/60))


## Known limitations

- **An incineration facility's residue carries the city's waste
  composition**, exactly as the combustion diversion pathway's reject always
  has. Real incinerator bottom ash is far more inert than that, so the
  residue's methane is an overestimate. Giving the reject its own composition
  changes the diversion pathway too, not just `combusts`, so it wants its own
  change. ([#53](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/53))

- **The single-site `advanced_dst` path cannot model incineration.** Its
  `waste_mass` is documented as the final landfilled mass, so combustion
  belongs upstream of it; only `adst_city_level` grew `combusts`. The ADST
  frontend calls the city-level endpoint for both its city and site modes, so
  nothing user-facing depends on this. ([#53](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/53))

- **Collected gas that is vented is still oxidised as if it left through the
  cover.** It left through a stack. At `capture 0.6`, `oxidation 0.22` and
  `flaring 0`, the model gives `0.78 × generation` where the physical answer is
  `0.912 ×`. Only reachable by setting `flaring` near zero, which the 0.98
  default makes rare; pinned by a test so it stays a decision rather than an
  accident. ([#51](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/51))

- **The two engines still give `captured` different meanings** — collected gas
  in the annual path (`generation × efficiency`), destroyed gas in the monthly
  one (`× flaring` as well). Unifying that changes a reported quantity with
  consumers outside this repo, so it wants its own change.
  ([#51](https://github.com/RMI/SWEET_python/pull/51))
